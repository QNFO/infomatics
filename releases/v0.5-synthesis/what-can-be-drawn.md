## Abstract

We present a systematic evidence-based survey of claims about "information as fundamental substrate" across 72 statements drawn from 35 external physics sources and 37 QNFO-internal papers. Using a classification framework grounded in Spencer-Brown's Laws of Form (1969) — where the act of measurement IS the act of drawing a distinction — we assign each claim one of five classes: DRAWN (measured by ≥2 independent metrological acts), DEFINED (mathematically specified but not yet measured), INFERRED (logically required by parent distinctions), HORIZON (below current metrology resolution), or QNFO (internally consistent, no external validation). Key finding: across all 72 claims, only 2 are DRAWN — the fine-structure constant α ≈ 1/137.035999084(21) (CODATA, measured by 4+ independent methods) and the absence of electron internal structure above 10⁻¹⁸ m (LEP). A further 28 claims are DEFINED in the external literature, while 37 claims exist only within the QNFO ecosystem with zero non-QNFO citations. The survey constructs an ultrametric distinction tree with α as the root organizing node, maps every claim to a metrology scale and tree depth, and identifies legitimate open problems (HORIZON distinctions) separable from QNFO-internal speculation. The paper concludes with recommendations for making QNFO research externally visible — starting with the ZBW-Majorana-TQC program's falsifiable experimental protocols, which represent the most testable bridge between QNFO theory and external metrology.

## §1. Introduction

### 1.1 Motivation

Since Wheeler's "it from bit" essay (1989), the proposition that information might be more fundamental than matter, energy, space, and time has attracted sustained attention across theoretical physics, quantum information theory, and the foundations of quantum mechanics. The QNFO research collective has produced approximately 35 papers exploring this thesis from multiple angles: Zitterbewegung as a p-adic observable (7 papers), ultrametric physics (15+ papers), α as a geometric cross-ratio, toroidal electron models, adelic quantum error correction, and an axiomatic Infomatics framework.

A central challenge in assessing this body of work is distinguishing between claims that have external validation — peer review, experimental evidence, citations from independent groups — and claims that are internally consistent but invisible to the broader scientific community. This survey provides that assessment.

### 1.2 Classification Framework

We adopt a classification framework grounded in **Spencer-Brown's Laws of Form** (1969). The primitive act in Laws of Form is: "Draw a distinction." The act of drawing a distinction creates an inside, an outside, and a boundary that separates them. Translating this to metrology: **the act of measurement IS the act of drawing a distinction.**

From this foundation, we define five distinction classes:

| Class | Definition | Metrology Translation |
|:------|:-----------|:----------------------|
| **DRAWN** | ≥2 independent metrological acts have drawn this distinction | Measured, reproducible, confirmed by different methods |
| **DEFINED** | Mathematically specified at a known scale; apparatus proposed but not yet operated | Theory exists; measurement is feasible but not yet executed |
| **INFERRED** | Logically required by the tree structure given parent distinctions that ARE drawn | Must exist (like the Planck length from ħ, G, c) but cannot be directly measured |
| **HORIZON** | Below current metrology floor; structure must exist per Laws of Form but is unlabeled | Physics we know is there but cannot resolve |
| **QNFO** | Exists within QNFO framework; no external group has drawn, defined, or inferred this distinction | Internally consistent, externally invisible |

For cross-referencing with the external literature, each claim also receives an **external recognition tag**: Established (≥3 independent groups, ≥100 citations), Active (≥2 groups discussing), Noted (≥1 citation), or None (zero non-QNFO citations).

## §2. Methodology

### 2.1 External Literature Search

We conducted 14 keyword searches across the arXiv API covering seven claim domains: Wheeler's "it from bit" (D1), Zitterbewegung experimental status (D2), the fine-structure constant α as a geometric ratio (D3), electron internal structure (D4), p-adic and ultrametric connections to physics (D5), the adelic framework (D6), and information-theoretic derivations of quantum field theory (D7). All 65 returned papers were verified against the arXiv API and classified by abstract content. Zero citations were generated from LLM training data.

### 2.2 QNFO-Internal Audit

We cross-referenced the seven claim domains against the QNFO Knowledge Graph (2,141 nodes), the D1 living-paper database (619 papers), and the QNFO Vectorize semantic index. We identified approximately 35 QNFO papers making claims in these domains and verified their external citation counts via DOI resolution and Semantic Scholar.

### 2.3 Classification Process

Every claim — whether external or QNFO — was classified by the same five-class system, assigned a metrology scale, a tree depth relative to α (the root distinction), and a parent distinction.

### 3.2 The Two DRAWN Distinctions

Across all seven domains and 72 claims, only **two** distinctions have been DRAWN — measured by independent metrological acts:

1. **α ≈ 1/137.035999084(21)** — the fine-structure constant, measured by four independent methods (electron g-2, rubidium atom recoil, cesium atom recoil, and CODATA combined fit) to 0.15 parts per billion precision. This is the ROOT distinction of the entire electromagnetic sector tree.

2. **No electron internal structure above 10⁻¹⁸ m** — the LEP electron-positron collider established that the electron shows no internal structure at momentum transfers below approximately 100 GeV, corresponding to a spatial resolution of ~10⁻¹⁸ m.

Every other claim across all domains — the ZBW, the p-adic connection, the adelic Dirac equation, the toroidal electron geometry, the cross-ratio reframing of α — is DEFINED (mathematically specified), HORIZON (below metrology), or QNFO (internally consistent but externally invisible).

### 3.3 The QNFO Citation Gap

All 37 QNFO claims share a common property: **zero non-QNFO citations.** The ZBW-Majorana-TQC program (7 papers with Zenodo DOIs), the Ultrametric Physics modules (15+ papers), the Cross-Ratio reframing of α, the α-π-Helix project, the Adelic QEC framework, the Syntactic Token Calculus — all published on Zenodo, all internally cross-referenced, **none cited by a single external physicist.**

This does not mean the work is wrong. It means the work is invisible. QNFO operates in a self-contained citation ecosystem that does not intersect with the broader physics literature.

## §4. Discussion

### 4.1 The Measurement Horizon

The distinction tree reveals a sharp boundary: everything above the DRAWN line is established physics. Everything below it is DEFINED, INFERRED, or HORIZON. The QNFO ecosystem operates almost entirely below this line — in the space of distinctions that are mathematically specified but not yet drawn by metrology.

This is not inherently problematic. Theoretical physics routinely operates in DEFINED space (string theory, quantum gravity, GUTs). The challenge for QNFO is not that its claims are DEFINED rather than DRAWN — it is that they are DEFINED in a **self-contained citation ecosystem** with no bridges to external validation.

### 4.2 The α Distinction

The fine-structure constant occupies a unique position. It is the ONLY distinction in this entire survey that is both DRAWN and the root of an entire distinction tree. Every other claim about electron structure, ZBW, or information-theoretic physics is a child of α.

The question "why α ≈ 1/137.036?" emerges from the tree structure as the question: "What determines the branching factor at this particular node in the ultrametric hierarchy?" This is a legitimate question, but it is not a question that can be answered by reframing α as a cross-ratio or proclaiming 137 to be prime. It is a question about the STRUCTURE of the tree — and answering it requires either (a) deriving α from a deeper theory or (b) accepting that α is a measured parameter and investigating what it measures.

### 4.3 ZBW as Re-Entry

In Laws of Form, the re-entering equation f = ┐f┌ generates an oscillation. This oscillation is the minimal generator of time — before re-entry, the calculus is atemporal. We note a structural isomorphism: the Zitterbewegung oscillation at frequency ω = 2mc²/ħ is a re-entering form. The positive and negative energy components of the Dirac spinor re-enter each other's indicational space, producing an oscillation whose spatial period is the Compton wavelength λ_C = ħ/(mc).

This structural isomorphism does not constitute a physical DRAWN distinction — it is an INFERRED connection between the Laws of Form calculus and the Dirac equation. It becomes a DEFINED claim if and when a measurement protocol can distinguish between the standard Dirac interpretation and the re-entry interpretation.

### 4.4 The Path to External Visibility

The ZBW-Majorana-TQC program is the most promising bridge to external visibility. It has:

1. **Specific falsification conditions** (P3: three experimental protocols with quantitative thresholds)
2. **A clear chain of inference** (P1: ZBW graph is ultrametric → P2: correlator is Z₂ invariant → P3: measurable by spin noise/EELS)
3. **Existing external apparatus** (Gerritsma 2010 trapped-ion simulator, Guo et al. 2025 vortex-enhanced ZBW)
4. **An established mathematical foundation** (Dragovich 2003 p-adic QM, well-cited externally)

If any of the P3 protocols produces a positive result in a peer-reviewed venue with external co-authors, the program crosses from QNFO-Falsifiable to DEFINED (External-Active). This is the single highest-leverage action for the QNFO ecosystem.

## §5. Conclusion

### 5.1 Summary of Findings

1. **Only 2 of 72 claims are DRAWN.** The fine-structure constant and the LEP electron structure bound are the only distinctions confirmed by independent metrology.

2. **37 claims are QNFO-only with zero external citations.** This is not a judgment of quality — it is a measurement of visibility.

3. **The distinction tree is organized by α.** Every electromagnetic claim descends from this single DRAWN root.

4. **QNFO's most testable program is ZBW-Majorana-TQC.** The Bruhat-Tits readout protocols (P3) define specific falsification conditions that external groups could execute with existing apparatus.

5. **The 2025 external toroidal electron model** independently converges on QNFO geometry, suggesting the toroidal ansatz is not unique to QNFO.

### 5.2 Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|:---------|:-------|:----------|
| **P0** | Submit P3 experimental protocol to an external collaboration | The ZBW-Majorana program's falsification conditions are the most testable bridge to external validation |
| **P1** | Cross-reference α-π-Helix predictions with 2025 external toroidal model | Independent convergence on toroidal geometry → opportunity for joint publication |
| **P2** | Map QNFO papers to external citation targets | Every QNFO paper with a Zenodo DOI should be cited by at least one external paper; identify those papers and send preprints |
| **P3** | Publish this survey in a peer-reviewed venue | The classification matrix itself is a novel contribution — a systematic evidence map of an underexplored research frontier |

### 5.3 Limitations

This survey is limited to arXiv as the primary external search source. Future work should extend to INSPIRE-HEP, Web of Science, and Scopus for comprehensive citation tracking. The QNFO-internal paper count (~35) is approximate due to the distributed nature of QNFO publications across Zenodo, D1, and the Knowledge Graph.
